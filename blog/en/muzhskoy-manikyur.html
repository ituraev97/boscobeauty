Men's manicure: what it involves, why bother and what it costs in Tashkent

August 4, 2026·Reading ~6 min·Nails

A men's manicure is hygienic work on the nails and the skin around them, with no polish and no decoration. It takes 30 to 50 minutes, costs roughly 120,000–250,000 UZS across the city — about $10–20 — and exists for the same reason a haircut does: so your hands look looked-after and nothing catches. What a men's manicure is

Correcting the length and shape of the nails, working on the overgrown skin around them, and softening any hardened patches on the fingers. No colour. If you want it, a matte clear coat goes on at the end — invisible, and it protects the nail from splitting.

The point is order rather than decoration. A properly finished nail does not split or catch on things, and the skin around it does not tear — those tears being the most common cause of soreness and inflammation.

How it differs from a women's manicure

Technically, almost not at all: the same e-file, the same sequence. The difference is in emphasis.

  • Length and shape. Nails are cut short and the edges rounded, so they do not interfere with grip.
  • Skin. Men's cuticle tends to be denser and grows back faster, which is exactly where e-file work pays off.
  • Coating. None by default. Most people leave with bare nails; some choose a matte clear coat.
  • Time. Without decoration the appointment is shorter.

BOSCO works with women and men alike. There is no separate men's booking and no special arrangement — it is an ordinary service.

How the appointment goes

  1. Disinfection. The technician treats their hands and yours.
  2. Shape. Edges are brought to the length you want.
  3. Skin. The e-file removes the overgrown layer and tidies any torn areas.
  4. Fingers. Hardened patches are treated where needed.
  5. Finish. The plate is buffed, cream or oil goes on the skin, and a clear coat if you asked for one.

Soaking bowls are usually skipped: the dry e-file technique is more hygienic and does not soften the skin, so the result lasts longer.

Worth spelling out: with an e-file nothing is cut, which means the risk of nicks is low. That matters for first-timers — most people picture a manicure as scissors and nippers.

Thirty to fifty minutes. A first visit can run longer if the skin around the nails has not been dealt with for a while.

Men's pedicure: when it is worth it

A pedicure often turns out to be the more useful of the two, especially if you spend the day in closed shoes or train regularly.

Concrete reasons: thickened skin on the heel, calluses at friction points, hard and thickened nails, or a nail edge starting to press into the side fold. Do not leave that last one — it is how an ingrown nail begins.

It does not have to be combined with a manicure; these are separate services. But putting them in one visit is convenient, and the total comes to less than two separate trips.

How often to go

Every three to four weeks on average. The interval depends on how fast your nails grow and how much you use your hands.

If you handle tools, type all day or train, the interval shortens: skin hardens faster under load. Tashkent's dry air adds to it, so a simple hand cream in the evenings between visits genuinely extends the result. Wearing gloves for household cleaning helps too — detergents dry the skin faster than the work does.

Your first time: what to expect

The main barrier before a first visit is not the appointment itself but the sense that it is not for you. In practice that resolves in the first five minutes: you sit down, the technician asks what you do and what is bothering you, and starts working.

Useful to know in advance:

  • It does not hurt. E-file work feels like a light vibration and does not cut the skin.
  • Nobody will push polish or decoration on you — it is not part of the standard service.
  • You can ask for more or less length; say so at the start.
  • If a shared floor feels awkward, ask when the salon is quietest.
  • Tools should come out of a sealed pouch in front of you. Asking about that is entirely normal.

Booking works the same way as booking a barber, and it is tied to a specific technician. If you get on with one, you can book them again next time — that is standard practice.

No preparation is needed: do not cut or soak anything beforehand. Quite the opposite, it is easier for the technician to see the starting point.

Frequently asked questions

Do men get polish?

Not by default. The standard appointment finishes bare. If you want it, a matte clear coat can be applied — it is not visible and protects the nail from splitting.

How long does a men's manicure take?

Usually 30 to 50 minutes, or 40 to 60 with a clear coat. A first visit can run longer if the skin around the nails has not been dealt with for a while.

How often should a man get a manicure?

Every three to four weeks on average. If you use your hands heavily or train regularly, the interval shortens, because the skin hardens faster under load.

What does a men's manicure cost in Tashkent?

City averages run 120,000–250,000 UZS without coating and 250,000–450,000 UZS for a men's pedicure, roughly $10–36. The spread reflects the technique and the technician's experience.

Do I have to book a pedicure at the same time?

No, they are separate services. Combining them in a single visit is convenient, though: the total time comes to less than two separate appointments.
