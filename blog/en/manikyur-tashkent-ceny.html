The types of manicure

A manicure is not just polish. First and foremost it is work on the nail and the skin around it; colour is the optional part. By technique, salons here offer a few distinct approaches.

  • E-file manicure. Done dry with a rotating bit. Nothing is cut, so the risk of nicks is low and the skin around the nail hardens more slowly.
  • Combined manicure. The bulk of the work is done with the e-file, the awkward corners by hand. This is the most common option in Tashkent salons today.
  • Classic manicure. Done entirely by hand with cutting tools; the skin is softened first, then trimmed. The oldest method, still widely offered, with a higher chance of nicks.
  • File-only manicure. Nothing but a file: length and shape are corrected, the skin is left alone. The gentlest option.

No method is inherently better than another. The difference is in speed, safety and how long the result holds.

E-file or combined: which to choose

The e-file method works dry — no soaking bowls, no softening. The layer under the nail plate does not swell, so gel polish sits flatter and lasts longer.

The combined method suits you better if there is a lot of overgrown skin, or if the skin around your nails tends to tear. The e-file takes off the bulk, the technician finishes the rest by hand. For most people this is the sensible default.

It is fine to leave the choice to the technician: they will look at the state of your nails and skin and tell you which approach fits. If it is your first visit, say so when you book — a little extra time gets set aside.

How long gel polish lasts

Done well, gel polish holds for two to three weeks. After that the nail has grown out and the line at the cuticle becomes visible — not because the coating failed.

What shortens it is mundane: housework without gloves, opening packaging with your nails, very hot water and frequent hand sanitiser. Tashkent's dry air adds to it — the skin around the nail dries out faster here, so a daily oil or cream genuinely helps.

If the coating chips within a week, the cause is almost always in the prep: the plate was not dried properly, or the edges were not sealed. That is a fair question to put to your technician.

How to tell the technician works safely

You do not need to be an expert. Several signs are visible in the first fifteen minutes.

  • Tools come out of a sealed pouch in front of you. That is the single clearest indicator of proper sterilisation.
  • The technician disinfects their hands and yours before starting.
  • The workstation is wiped down after every guest and waste is binned where you can see it.
  • Your skin is not damaged. Bleeding during a manicure is not normal.
  • There is no sharp chemical smell in the room, which means extraction and ventilation are working.

Asking how tools are processed is entirely normal. A salon confident in its work answers calmly and will show you if asked.

How much does a manicure cost in Tashkent?

City averages run 100,000–200,000 UZS without polish and 150,000–350,000 UZS with gel polish, roughly $8–28. The spread reflects the technician's experience and the brand of products used. BOSCO's current prices are on the nail services page.

What is the difference between an e-file and a combined manicure?

An e-file manicure is done entirely dry with rotating bits. A combined manicure uses the e-file for the bulk of the work and hand tools for the awkward corners. The combined approach is more comfortable when there is a lot of overgrown skin.

How often should I get a manicure?

Usually every two to three weeks, which is how long it takes for the regrowth line to become noticeable. If you wear no coating, once a month for upkeep is enough.

Does gel polish ruin your nails?

The coating itself does not; removing it badly does. Scraping or peeling it off takes the top layer of the plate with it. Properly soaked off, gel polish does not thin the nail.
