Hair care in Tashkent: coping with hard water, sun and very dry air

August 4, 2026·Reading ~7 min·Hair

Three things make hair care in Tashkent harder than it was wherever you moved from: hard tap water, a brutal summer sun and extremely dry air — with central heating added to that in winter. The good news is that almost all of it is offset by how you wash rather than by expensive products. BOSCO's current prices are on the hair services page.

What hard water does to hair

Tashkent tap water carries a lot of calcium and magnesium salts. As you wash, they settle on the hair shaft as a thin film that ordinary rinsing does not remove.

The symptoms are recognisable: hair feels rough, loses shine, tangles near the roots and combs badly. Shampoo lathers worse, so you instinctively pour more — and the excess dries out your scalp further.

Colour-treated hair is a separate case. The mineral film changes how light reflects off the strand, so cool tones turn brassy faster and rich shades look dusty within two or three weeks. If you arrived with a salon colour done elsewhere, expect it to shift sooner here than it did at home.

What helps in practice:

  • A shower filter. Not a cure, but the difference in combing is noticeable from the first wash.
  • Conditioner after every wash: it balances pH and closes the cuticle that alkaline water lifts.
  • A clarifying shampoo every two or three weeks to strip the accumulated film.
  • A final rinse in cool water.

Sun and heat: the Tashkent summer problem

From June to September your scalp takes as much ultraviolet as exposed skin — but nobody has the habit of protecting it. UV breaks down the protein structure of the strand and bleaches out pigment.

By the end of summer the outcome is much the same for everyone: colour lifted a tone or two, ends dry and brittle, lengths drier than roots. On coloured hair it shows more, because lightened lengths lose moisture faster.

The minimum kit: a leave-in with a UV filter on the lengths, a hat during the hottest hours, and always washing after the pool — chlorine combined with sun is particularly harsh.

One more summer habit worth breaking: do not tie wet hair into a tight ponytail. Damp hair stretches and snaps more easily, and in this heat it stays wet under the elastic for hours.

Heating season and dry air

In winter the problem changes shape rather than going away. Air in heated rooms gets very dry and the lengths give up moisture to it.

Hence the familiar winter complaints: static, frizz, strands escaping the style, hair looking thinner than it did in summer. Add a hat that the lengths rub against, and the constant temperature swing every time you step indoors.

Simple things work: a humidifier at home, a leave-in cream or oil on the lengths before going out, a hat with a smooth lining instead of coarse wool. And do not blast hair with hot air on the highest setting — a medium setting finished with a cool shot gives a far smoother result.

The routine that actually works

Order matters more than the number of bottles. A working sequence looks like this.

  1. Shampoo on the scalp only. The lengths get cleaned by the lather running down; scrubbing them separately is unnecessary.
  2. Conditioner or mask on the lengths only, starting a couple of centimetres from the roots.
  3. Comb while wet, with conditioner in, using a wide-tooth comb, working from the ends upward.
  4. Leave-in product on damp lengths: it eases combing and protects during drying.
  5. Heat protection before a dryer or straightener — every time, no exceptions.

Water temperature matters too. Hot water lifts the cuticle and strips oil aggressively, after which the scalp produces more of it. Wash warm, rinse cool.

A mask does not replace conditioner, and it does not work better for being left on an hour instead of five minutes. Consistency beats intensity: once a week for a month does more than one heroic session.

When home care is no longer enough

A few signals mean changing shampoo will not fix it.

  • Hair snaps along the whole length while combing, not just at the ends.
  • After bleaching or chemical straightening the lengths stretch like gum when wet.
  • The scalp itches, flakes, or has sore patches.
  • Density has visibly dropped and the parting has widened.

The first two are a job for a salon. The last two are a reason to see a doctor first: both scalp conditions and shedding are more often tied to what is happening in the body than to your products.

Salon treatments do not "heal" hair, but they solve specific problems faster than home care can. A clarifying treatment removes the mineral film so your products start working again; restorative treatments bring back density and shine for a few weeks; a trim removes what physically cannot be repaired. Myths worth dropping

"Hair gets used to a shampoo." It is not living tissue and cannot get used to anything. The feeling that a shampoo "stopped working" usually means silicone or mineral build-up.

"Frequent trims make hair grow faster." Hair grows from the follicle; the ends have no say in the rate. Trimming simply stops the lengths thinning out and breaking.

"Cold water closes the pores." There are no pores on a hair shaft, there are cuticle scales. Cool water does smooth them — the effect is real, the explanation is not.

"Oil repairs dry hair." Oil smooths the surface and adds shine but does not add moisture from within. It belongs at the end of a routine, not as the whole of it.

Frequently asked questions

Does a shower filter help?

Partly. A filter reduces the calcium and magnesium content and hair combs noticeably more easily after washing. It cannot fully soften the water, so treat it as an addition to your routine rather than a replacement for it.

How often should I wash my hair in hard water?

As often as your scalp needs. Washing less does not reduce the effect of hard water, and excess oil holds the mineral film in place. Using conditioner after every wash matters more than stretching the intervals.

Does hair need SPF in summer?

In a Tashkent summer, yes. UV degrades pigment and the protein structure of the strand, which is why colour lifts and ends turn brittle. A leave-in with a UV filter plus a hat is enough.

Is it true that frequent washing causes hair loss?

No. Washing does not change how many hairs are in the shedding phase — it simply collects the ones that have already detached from the follicle. If shedding has genuinely increased, the cause lies elsewhere.

How do I know my hair needs a cut rather than treatment?

The clearest sign is ends that tangle and look thin, frizz straight after washing and do not respond to masks. A split end cannot be physically repaired; it can only be cut off.
