How to choose a beauty salon in Tashkent: nine signs you are in good hands

August 4, 2026·Reading ~7 min·Useful

Choosing a salon in a city you have just moved to is mostly guesswork from photos. It does not have to be. What matters is not the interior: it is how tools are sterilised, whether the price is quoted before the work starts, and whether anyone talks to you first. Here are nine things visible on a first visit. BOSCO's services and current prices are on the home page.

Sterilisation: the question to ask

This is the first point and the most important. Manicures and pedicures involve contact with skin, so tool sterilisation here is not a matter of tidiness but of safety.

  • Tools come out of a sealed pouch in front of you. That is the clearest single indicator.
  • A separate set per guest. One set is not used on several people in a row.
  • Single-use materials: file covers, wipes, liners for foot baths.
  • The technician disinfects their hands and yours before starting.

Asking how tools are processed is completely normal, and it is worth doing even if your Uzbek or Russian is limited — the question translates easily and the answer is usually demonstrable. A salon confident in its work responds calmly and will show you. Deflection or a vague answer is itself an answer.

How to read reviews and ratings

An overall score tells you little on its own. What is useful is the text and how the reviews are distributed over time.

A good sign: reviews arrive steadily, some are detailed and middling rather than ecstatic, and the salon replies to criticism calmly. A bad sign: dozens of short five-star lines posted on the same day, and rude responses to anything negative.

Note what is being praised. "Beautiful interior" and "nice music" say nothing about the work. "The coating lasted three weeks" and "the ingrown nail stopped hurting" do.

In Tashkent, 2GIS is the map service people actually use rather than Google Maps, so that is where the reviews live. BOSCO has a 4.9 rating there and a 2GIS Awards nomination — but a rating is never the final proof on its own; check it against specific reviews.

Price transparency

A good sign is a published price list and a total quoted before the work begins, not after. A bad sign is a price that "depends" and gets clarified once you are already in the chair.

Ask in advance whether removal of the old coating, strengthening, nail art and shaping are included. Those are the items that most often appear on top at the till.

City averages for orientation: a manicure with gel polish runs 150,000–350,000 UZS (~$12–28), brow lamination with tint 200,000–350,000 UZS (~$16–28), a haircut 150,000–350,000 UZS. Current prices are published on the service pages: nails, brows and lashes, hair, podiatry.

The technician's work, not the salon's

Work posted on a salon's main account may belong to anyone, including someone who no longer works there. Ask to see the portfolio of the specific person you are booking.

Good work shows different hands, different lengths and different briefs rather than the same angle and the same design repeated. That tells you the technician has range, not one memorised look.

The conversation before the service

A good technician does not start immediately. They look at the state of your hands or hair, ask what is bothering you, and explain what they propose to do.

That is not wasted time. It is the stage where it becomes clear whether the service you booked is right for you, or whether something else makes more sense today. Being talked out of a service that does not suit you is a good sign, not a bad one.

A useful question to ask: "What would you recommend?" Watch what follows. Being asked specific questions in return — what you do, how long the last coating lasted, any allergies — is a good sign. Being offered the most expensive option immediately is not.

Cleanliness and smell

The workstation is wiped down after every guest, waste is binned where you can see it, and there is nothing left on the floor from the previous appointment.

Smell is a separate indicator. There should be no sharp chemical odour in the room; if you notice one, extraction and ventilation are not doing their job. In a Tashkent summer that matters more than usual — with the windows shut and the air conditioning on, the air goes stale quickly.

Online booking and punctuality

Online booking is not a guarantee of quality in itself, but it says something about the operation: the salon accounts for time and plans its flow of guests. For anyone whose Russian or Uzbek is limited, it also removes the phone call from the process entirely.

Punctuality is the stronger signal. Half an hour's wait past your slot can be coincidence, but if it happens every time, the schedule is built on optimism rather than real timings.

What to do if you are unhappy

Say so immediately, before you leave. A great deal can be corrected on the spot: shape, length, tone. Once you are home it is considerably harder.

If the problem is technical — the coating chipped within a week, the nail split — tell the salon. A reasonable one will put it right. If the answer is "that is just your nails" and nothing else, that is also an answer.

Red flags

  • Tools taken from a communal container, or a pouch opened out of your sight.
  • A vague answer to the sterilisation question.
  • The price only becomes clear after the work is done.
  • A price far below the market — almost always a saving made on materials or on safety.
  • The technician damages your skin and treats that as normal.
  • Heavy pressure to add services you did not ask for.
  • Social media work that looks suspiciously like other salons' posts.

One flag is not a verdict. Two or three together are a reason to look elsewhere.

Frequently asked questions

How can I check that tools are sterilised?

The simplest way is to watch whether tools come out of a sealed pouch in front of you. Single-use materials and a separate set per guest matter too. Asking how tools are processed is entirely normal.

What does a good manicure cost in Tashkent?

City averages run 150,000–350,000 UZS with gel polish at an experienced technician, roughly $12–28. A price far below the market usually means savings on materials or safety. A high price is no guarantee of quality on its own — look at the work and the organisation.

Can I trust 2GIS ratings?

As a starting point, yes, but not as final proof. Read the text of the reviews rather than the overall score: the ones describing a specific result are more useful than the ones describing the interior.

Can I come for a consultation without booking?

At most salons you can, but it is better to message ahead so time is set aside and you are not left waiting. Telegram is the easiest way to arrange it.

What if I do not like the result?

Say so before you leave: shape, length and tone can all be adjusted on the spot. If the problem appears later — a coating that chips within a week, for instance — tell the salon. A reasonable one will put it right.
